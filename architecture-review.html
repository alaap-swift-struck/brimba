The blast-radius map

No fallback, on purpose. A short cache of “this session is valid” would survive a wobble and earn the last 15 points of criterion 2. It would also let a revoked session — a departing employee, a stolen laptop — keep working for the length of that cache. Revocation is instant today; the owner chose on 12 August 2026 to keep it that way. Recorded in ARCHITECTURE §2b so the trade stays visible.

The restore drill — proven, not documented

Criterion 6 was capped at 55 because nothing recorded an actual restore test. One was run on a throwaway database: two rows written, a bookmark taken, DELETE FROM invoices confirming zero rows, then a restore. Both rows came back with identical values. The drill database was deleted afterwards; no real data was touched.

It found two faults in the runbook, which is the entire reason for running one:

  • The documented time-travel info --remote is not a valid command — that flag does not exist on that subcommand. The runbook would have failed in the one moment it mattered.
  • Only --timestamp was documented. The bookmark form is the precise one, and it is what the tool actually hands you.

Now also stated: the window is 30 days on Workers Paid, 7 on Free — and anything older is not recoverable by any means, which nothing said before.
